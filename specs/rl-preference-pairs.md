## Notation
- $\tau_c$ — the fresh episode of cycle $c$: all recorded real states $0..T$, not only the states with executed actions. A state reference $x=(episode,index)$ identifies its raw situation and enough raw past to reconstruct every required history. Two references are equal iff their episode and index are equal.
- $\mathcal H$ — persistent History. Its **vertices are seed states only**, and every stored comparison partner is also a History vertex. A vertex holds its state reference, insertion-time latent key $z^{key}$, comparison records and usage statistics. Ordinary non-seed episode states never enter candidate sets, comparison records or $Q_c$.
- A comparison record on a vertex $u$ refers to another History vertex $v$ and holds $(\bar p_{u,v},\bar p_{inc,u,v},n_{u,v})$: arithmetic mean preference for the ordered pair $(u,v)$, arithmetic mean incomparability and the number of comparisons. The record on $v$ is its symmetric counterpart.
- $n_u=\sum_v n_{u,v}$ over the records currently attached to $u$; $a_u$ — the number of completed sampling cycles in which $u$ was not actually compared. Selection as a candidate without a comparison does not reset $a_u$.
- $N_s$ = `sample_states`, $N_t$ = `temporal_per_state`, $N_n$ = `nearest_per_state`, $B$ = `branch`, $W$ = `beam_width`, $D$ = `beam_depth`, $Q_{max}$ = `capacity`.
- $\tau_0$ = `base_threshold`, in $(0,1)$; $\lambda$ = `temporal_penalty`, $\ge0$; $\kappa$ = `threshold_scale`, in $(0,1)$.
- For a comparison $P(Y_u,Y_v)$ returning logits $(s,u_{inc})$: $p=\sigma(s)$, $q=\sigma(u_{inc})$ with `torch.sigmoid`. For scalar thresholds, $\sigma_0(x)=1/(1+\exp(-x))$, evaluated in Python float64 as `1 / (1 + math.exp(-x))`. For a current comparable comparison, $w=\max(p,1-p)$; the arc goes from the preferred state to the other one, with the seed first when $p=1/2$.
- $d_\alpha(x,y)=\|\alpha\odot(x-y)\|_2$ on insertion-time latent keys, even when those keys come from different versions of $\phi$.
- Every random draw uses the caller's `numpy.random.Generator`, checkpointed with the cycle. Stable-id ordering means lexicographic $(episode,index)$ ordering.

## Math
(1) Auxiliary seed scores [U]: for every episode state $t=1..T$, compute $P(Y_t,Y_{t-1})$ by the start-of-cycle $\phi,P$ in eval mode under `torch.no_grad`; with its $p_t,q_t$,
$score_t=\max\left(q_t,(1-q_t)|2p_t-1|\right)$.
For $t=0$, $score_0=0$. These comparisons are used only here: they produce no History record, corrected pair, label or training item.

(2) Seed sampling [U]: $n_s=\min(N_s,T+1)$. Let $U_+=\{t:score_t>0\}$ and $U_0=\{t:score_t=0\}$. First draw $n_+=\min(n_s,|U_+|)$ distinct states of $U_+$ without replacement with probabilities $score_t/\sum_{j\in U_+}score_j$, in the draw's returned order. If $n_+<n_s$, then draw $n_s-n_+$ distinct states of $U_0$ uniformly without replacement and append them in the draw's returned order. Thus an all-zero episode is a uniform draw, while insufficient positive scores never reduce $n_s$ and are filled from zero-score states. A zero-sized draw is skipped without an RNG call. Stage all seeds in the pending History before choosing any alternatives; each starts with no comparison records, $n_u=0,a_u=0$ and a provisional selection key computed from its raw history by the start-of-cycle $\phi$ in eval mode under `torch.no_grad`. This pending state is reconstructible work, not a published generation. An episode with no states produces no work.

(3) Temporal alternatives [U]: for seed $u$, every other seed $v$ of the fresh episode has weight $|t_u-t_v|$; sample $\min(N_t,n_s-1)$ distinct alternatives without replacement from
$Pr(v\mid u)=|t_u-t_v|/\sum_{x\ne u,\;x\;is\ a\ seed}|t_u-t_x|$.
Keep the draw's returned order as their discovery order. Only these other seed vertices may be temporal alternatives. With fewer than two seeds or a zero-sized draw this set is empty and no RNG call is made.

(4) Nearest alternatives [U]: among current History vertices outside the fresh episode, take the $\min(N_n,count)$ smallest $d_\alpha(z^{key}_u,z^{key}_v)$, ordered by $(distance,stable\ id)$. Historical keys are not recomputed. With no such vertices the set and its beam are empty.

(5) Polarity [U]: for a stored comparison between History vertices $x,y$,
$J(x,y)=\max\left(\bar p_{inc,x,y},(1-\bar p_{inc,x,y})|2\bar p_{x,y}-1|\right)$.
It is orientation-invariant and selects highly incomparable edges as well as comparable edges with strongly positive or negative preference.

(6) Beam [U], independently for each seed: initial frontier = its ordered nearest alternatives; visited = {seed} ∪ frontier; alternatives = frontier. Repeat at most $D$ times:
1. For each frontier vertex $x$ in frontier order, rank its comparison records by descending $J(x,y)$, ties by the partner's stable id, excluding the current episode and visited vertices; take at most $B$.
2. For every candidate $y$, retain the largest $J$ of an edge that discovered it at this depth; order candidates by descending that value, ties by stable id. Take at most $W$ as the new frontier.
3. Add the new frontier to visited and to this seed's alternatives. Stop early if it is empty.
Only selected frontier vertices join the alternatives; discarded branch candidates do not. Temporal alternatives are not beam inputs.

(7) Current comparisons [U]: for every seed, compare it with the union of its temporal, nearest and beam alternatives, all of which are History vertices. Globally deduplicate unordered vertex pairs in seed-processing order and alternative-discovery order; the first occurrence fixes the orientation (its seed first). Each unique pair is compared once in this cycle by the start-of-cycle $\phi,P$ in eval mode under `torch.no_grad`. The result is staged in the pending History before correction:
$n_{u,v}\leftarrow n_{u,v}+1$,
$\bar p_{u,v}\leftarrow\bar p_{u,v}+(p-\bar p_{u,v})/n_{u,v}$,
$\bar p_{inc,u,v}\leftarrow\bar p_{inc,u,v}+(q-\bar p_{inc,u,v})/n_{u,v}$;
the reverse record uses $1-\bar p_{u,v}$ and the same $\bar p_{inc},n$. A vertex participating in at least one current comparison has $a_u=0$ in the pending state; aging of every other vertex is deferred to publication.

(8) Temporal threshold [U]: for two vertices of one episode with $d=|t_u-t_v|\ge1$,
$\theta(d)=\kappa\,\sigma_0\left(\operatorname{logit}(\tau_0)+\lambda/d\right)$.
For vertices of different episodes, $\theta=\kappa\tau_0$. The pair is initially incomparable iff $q>\theta$; otherwise it is comparable. In exact arithmetic, for $\lambda>0$, close pairs have a higher incomparability threshold: $\theta(d)$ decreases with $d$ and approaches $\kappa\tau_0$ from above. Thus at a fixed $q$, temporal proximity favours initial comparability. For $\lambda=0$, every threshold is $\kappa\tau_0$. In exact arithmetic the multiplier keeps every threshold below $\kappa<1$; float64 rounding may reach $\kappa$ or make nearby thresholds equal. This rule changes only the verdict, not $p,q$ or the graph arc costs.

(9) Correction [U]: build one directed graph from only the current cycle's unique comparisons. Every comparable pair contributes one arc from the preferred state with cost $w-1/2$; initially incomparable pairs contribute no arc. Apply `removed_arcs` of `specs/acyclic.md` (2), (3), preserving its vertex/arc ordering rules. A retained arc $(u,v)$ produces pair $(u,v)$ with labels `{"p": w, "inc": 0}` — the original probabilistic prediction $w$, not 1. A removed arc likewise produces its preferred-first pair $(u,v)$, with `{"p": null, "inc": 1}` as in `specs/acyclic.md` (4). An initially incomparable pair produces its original comparison pair with `{"p": null, "inc": 1}`. Historical means and old corrected datasets are not inputs to this correction.

(10) Cleanup [U], during publication after training: while the number of pending History vertices exceeds $Q_{max}$, compute for every vertex
$score_u=\sqrt{n_u/(a_u+1)}$.
Remove a vertex with the smallest score, breaking ties by larger $a_u$, then stable id. Delete every record referring to it from the other vertices and subtract each deleted record's count from those vertices' $n$; their $a$ is unchanged. Recompute affected scores and repeat until the size is at most $Q_{max}$.

(11) Freezing and training [U]: the corrected pairs and labels of (9) are the cycle's fixed preference dataset $Q_c$. Their raw state references never change during joint training; their histories are re-encoded by the then-current $\phi$ on every use. Historical latent keys, historical means and old labels are not training inputs. Preference items cause only preference loss, with gradients into $P,\phi$.

(12) Memory publication [U]: after joint training, re-encode every fresh selected seed by final $\phi$ in eval mode under `torch.no_grad`, replacing only its provisional selection key by the final insertion key $z^{key}$. Keys of older vertices never change. Then increment $a_u$ for every pending vertex without a current comparison, perform (10), and atomically publish the pending History as generation $c$. No ordinary non-seed state is inserted and no publication occurs before training.

## Domain
- $\phi,P$ used for auxiliary scores, provisional selection keys and current comparisons are the start-of-cycle versions, in eval mode under `torch.no_grad`; final insertion keys use final $\phi$ in the same mode. The caller's module modes are restored afterwards. Raw histories are finite and on the caller's device. Historical keys originate in older networks and remain unchanged. One `select`/`sample_pairs` invocation memoizes each referenced $Y$ for reuse across (1), (2) and (7), without changing the per-pair $P$ calls or their order. This cache is invocation-local, is not checkpointed and is discarded on return or interruption; resume and final-key publication construct fresh caches, with publication encoding only through the caller's final $\phi$.
- $N_s,N_t,N_n,B,W,D$ are non-negative integers and $Q_{max}$ a positive integer. Zero values disable their corresponding candidate source or beam expansion. Empty History, empty candidate sets and empty $Q_c$ are valid. A one-seed episode has no temporal alternatives but may still have nearest/beam alternatives from older History.
- Current-cycle deduplication treats $(u,v)$ and $(v,u)$ as one pair. Repeated comparisons in later cycles are allowed and averaged. Correction has no `dropped` verdict.

Each has the appropriate `<desc>: nothing to do` for zero remaining work because the input is empty and `<desc>: already done` when an earlier run completed it.

## Tests
- Auxiliary scores against sigmoid/polarity oracles: immediate predecessor only, $t=0$, incomparable and strong positive/negative cases, no History or $Q_c$ side effects. Seeded weighted positive-score draws without replacement, uniform zero-score filling when positives are insufficient, the all-zero case, skipped zero-sized RNG calls and deterministic processing order against the numpy branch in [N].
- Only seeds become vertices or comparison partners; temporal probabilities, counts and returned order among current seeds; stale keys remain unchanged; nearest distances and stable ties are hand-computed.
- Polarity's three extreme cases and neutral case; beam branching, global width, depth, deduplication, visited/current-episode exclusion, best-discovery-$J$ and early stop.
- Current-pair global deduplication and orientation; arithmetic means in both orientations, repeat counts, usage aging only after actual comparisons and no-gradient/current-network calls.
- Thresholds by hand: close/distant/cross-episode, decreasing threshold with distance for $\lambda>0$, same-episode thresholds above the cross-episode baseline, $\lambda=0$, the $\kappa$ cap, strict `>`; a fixed incomparability prediction can be comparable nearby and incomparable far away. Verdicts leave $p,q$ and arc costs unchanged. Arc `Fraction` conversion and vertex/arc order are fixed.
- Correction against `removed_arcs` and independent labels: retained soft $w$, preferred-first orientation of retained and removed arcs, removed/initially incomparable binary inc with null p, current-cycle-only graph and unchanged old data.
- Cleanup scores, ties, repeated removal, symmetric reference deletion and count subtraction; exact capacity boundary.
- Fixed raw references/labels under later network changes and eviction; preference-only training semantics. No published History change after `sample_pairs`; publication only after training, final-$\phi$ keys on fresh selected seeds only, unchanged old keys, deferred aging and per-cycle nearest/beam access to earlier cycles. Interruptions after a score shard, seed-work shard, correction shard, unfinished final-key publication and complete History generation: byte-identical continuation with no repeated side effects; $Q_c$ readable before memory publication and after eviction; config loader, fingerprints, completion and all progress lines/initial positions.
